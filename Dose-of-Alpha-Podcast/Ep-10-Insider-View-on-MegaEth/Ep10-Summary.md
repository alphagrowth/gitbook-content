# Insider View on MegaETH — Dose of Alpha Ep. 10

**Guest:** Paper Imperium — Economics Lead at MegaETH (former DAO governance / delegation portfolio manager at GFX Labs; economics degree; ex-archaeologist by trade)

## TL;DR

- **Role:** Economics lead at MegaETH; owns 6–12 month incentive design and strategy by "inverting problems" (set target TVL / transactions / market cap, then work backwards).
- **Core thesis:** Blockchains are empty "frontier land" — you can pay settlers to arrive, but they must engage in **productive activity** or you run out of money. Gas fees alone are never enough.
- **Monetization edge:** MegaETH has a native stablecoin (**USDM**), so dollar-denominated TVL is monetized **passively** (funding buybacks) even on low-activity days — decoupling revenue from raw transaction volume.
- **Incentive philosophy:** Prefer providing **stability/predictability** (e.g. Avon's guaranteed ~5% APY on USDM) over high APY. Stability is **cheaper to provide**, rare in the market, and attracts sticky users. Counter-cyclical: incentives fall as organic yield rises, rise as yield falls. Go slow to avoid gaming.
- **Market positioning:** Do NOT fight 80,000 DeFi forks over the crowded 15% DeFi stablecoin market or compete with JP Morgan / Visa / Bank of America. Run the **Tether playbook** — serve underserved markets (Venezuela, capital-controlled economies) where a "B+ product" beats an absent or terrible local one.
- **Discipline:** Invokes **circle of competence** (Warren Buffett) — the point is honesty about the line and staying inside it, not expanding it. Rejects the premise that a chain should become a VC or hedge fund.
- **Unique product enabled by real-time chain:** **Hit One** — micro-stakes (~$5–$100) random long/short at extreme random leverage (~400x–1000x) on majors, only possible due to tiny/fast blocks.
- **DAOs:** Terminal for many because teams drifted from open-source-software governance into being "really shitty hedge funds that don't hedge." DAOs work for true immutable protocols (Uniswap, Morpho), not for high-risk asset allocation.
- **AI:** Both bullish and bearish — AI ≈ an infinite supply of smart, articulate 22-year-old interns with **no life experience**. Trust it only with what you'd trust such a person.

### Incentive Design & The Avon / USDM Model
- **Problem to avoid:** TVL pump-and-collapse. Cited an unnamed chain that fell from ~$250M TVL to single-digit millions (~1%) while token market cap stayed ~$80–120M — attributed to thoughtless incentives or lack of useful apps.
- **Marginal thinking:** Pay for the *additional* dollar/user, not the ones already happy to be there.
- **Avon (dominant share of MegaETH TVL):**
  - Provides incentives on **USDM deposits** with a **guaranteed ~5% APY floor**.
  - **Counter-cyclical stabilizer:** organic yield up → incentive down; yield down → incentive up. Weans incentives off as market heats; kicks in during slowdowns.
- **Level matters, not just the number:** 20% yield attracts mercenary capital ("live by the sword, die by the sword"); ~5% is a modest **risk premium** over mainnet (SUSDS / Aave / Compound ~3.5–4%).
- **Maker DAO lesson:** Institutions valued Maker's **semi-fixed-rate predictability** (manual, conservative risk team, bias toward not moving) — knew borrowing costs, got weeks of notice to move $100–200M positions. Different users have different objectives; **stability is a product**.
- **DeFi Summer caution:** Similar counter-cyclical designs failed when moved **too fast** — became gameable, incentivizing TVL dumps. Slow adjustment is the safeguard.

### Homegrown vs. Brand-Name Apps
- Everything must **serve a user's problem / deliver value**.
- Differentiate by **stack position:**
  - **Lower/infra** (e.g. Avon) — delivers value to the chain; brand vs. homegrown matters less.
  - **Consumer-facing / user-touching** — homegrown is a moat because it's unique and unavailable elsewhere.
- MegaETH is "blessed" with consumer entertainment-finance apps: **Hit One**, **Euphoria** (upcoming), **Offshore** (launching that week).

### Monetization: Why Gas Is Not Enough
- Most L2s/chains monetize via **gas** — but nobody makes much; requires relentless daily transaction volume ("you got a great day, now do it again tomorrow").
- Gas is best framed as an **anti-spam tool**, not a revenue engine.
- **Block space is a commodity** — competes on price, likely always low or negative margin; hard to find a non-replicable structural advantage.
- **MegaETH's structural advantage:** native stablecoin **USDM** → dollar TVL monetized passively → revenue on slow days (summer, low DEX/perps activity) → **buybacks**; enables a broader metric set than volume alone.
- Other monetization models discussed:
  - **Katana / GSR "full reserve"** model — monetize **bridge assets** (Paper: Polygon shouldn't have backed down under pushback).
  - **Optimism "Red Hat" model** — free software + consulting fees; skeptical it scales unless many chains exist (big chains say "we have our own people").
  - Compared to **Conduit** — sequencer-as-a-service / rollup-as-a-service.

### USDM Mechanics
- USDM is **externally operated (a concession)**, not issued/run by MegaETH.
- Backed **100% by USDC and USDTB**.
- **USDTB** is yield-bearing (a wrapped/pedal-type instrument).
- **USDC** sits on **Coinbase**, which pays yield on USDC balances (nominal monthly fee).
- Regulatory nuance referenced: post-**CLARITY**-type framing → **"rewards token, not a yield token."**

### Frontier / Land Analogies (Chain = Empty Land)
- **Paper's analogy:** Opening the American West — block space = empty land; need settlers, can pay them to arrive, but they must do **productive things** or the money runs out. No natural resources (unlike Utah land with minerals/timber).
- Referenced **Russian boyars** measuring estates in "souls" (serfs) for tax/value.

### Competitive Strategy & Circle of Competence
- Won't enter a "knife fight with 80,000 DeFi forks" over a small market.
- **Stablecoin market breakdown:** ~15% is DeFi (Sky, Ethena, Frax, Bold, plus Circle/Tether); targeting only DeFi cedes 85% and enters the most competitive niche.
- On its own chain, USDM has a real edge as the **home currency**.
- **Circle (skeptical):** trying to out-compete payment networks / JP Morgan; likely acquired at a discount without new products.
- **Tether (playbook to emulate):** competes against **bad/absent financial products** in the developing world (Venezuela banks, gray-market lenders in Bangladesh, capital-controlled FX) — dominant on **Tron** because there are no on-chain USD otherwise.
- **B+ beats absent A+:** DeFi's promise is distribution to people who lack access; a "good enough" product wins where premium products can't reach.
- **Exotic-pair example:** MegaETH hosts a **yield-bearing Turkish Lira stablecoin backed by government paper**; USD/EUR forex spreads are ~1 pip (no edge), but exotic pairs run ~300 pips (real margin).
- **Circle of competence (Warren Buffett):** People wrongly obsess over *expanding* it — the real skill is **honesty about where the line is and staying inside it**. Applies to L2s copying unrelated businesses ("they succeeded in XYZ, why don't we?") without the skill or distribution.

### Real-Time-Only Products
- **Hit One** — consumer entertainment-finance ("pro wrestling of finance"):
  - User **randomly** goes long/short a **random major** (BTC, ETH, SOL) at **random extreme leverage (~400x–1000x)**, max stake ~$5–$100.
  - Hold ~2 minutes, riding the random walk; auto stop-out or cash-out.
  - Only possible with **very small, fast blocks** — impossible on Ethereum's 6–12s block times.
- **World Markets** — backend infra beneath Hit One: perps + lending + spot, **fully on-chain, no backend**. (**GMX** is also on MegaETH.)
- **Oracles:** embedded oracles plus **Chainlink** and **RedStone** active. Open question on whether **OEV auctions** can run at real-time speeds — flagged as a topic to discuss with RedStone.
- **Tech stack:** uses the **OP Stack** but has **no business relationship with Optimism/OP**.

### KPIs
- MegaETH has a public **KPI page** that **unlocks locked "mega" tokens** as milestones are hit (stablecoin float, continuous uptime, block size — many technical).
- KPIs beyond TVL/users now include **native asset issuance** and **migrated assets** ("the more assets you issue, 100% keeps you sticky").
- **Native app market cap** cited as the surprising/underrated KPI.

### DAOs — Diagnosis
- Original DAO fit: governing loose confederations building/marketing **open-source software** (email, HTTPS protocols; Uniswap immutable except fee switch/tiers; **Morpho** = software sold, curators operate as separate businesses).
- **Failure mode:** after minimal success, teams pivoted to acting like **high-risk, inefficient hedge funds that don't hedge** — people who couldn't get hired doing the real thing, centralizing control. DAOs are terrible at being bad hedge funds.
- **Prescription:** if going that direction, **centralize**, add real **risk controls**, and hire people who execute. Host's analogy: DAOs are like everyone on Facebook voting on every line of every bill.
- Verdict: DAOs will return, but only **fit to the right use** (true protocols).

### Rapid-Fire Takes

- **AI & security:** Not a technical/code-security expert (does financial/legal/operational due diligence). Main new risk: unqualified people "ChatGPT-ing their way" into legal/technical/financial arrangements.

## Key Takeaways / Conclusions

- **Revenue must survive slow days.** A native stablecoin turns idle dollar-TVL into passive, volume-independent revenue (→ buybacks); gas fees never suffice and block space is a commodity.
- **Sell stability, not yield.** Predictable, modest risk premiums (~5% floor, counter-cyclical) attract sticky capital cheaply; high-APY farming is mercenary and self-defeating. Move incentive parameters slowly to prevent gaming.
- **Pick uncontested markets.** Avoid the crowded 15% DeFi stablecoin niche and TradFi giants; win where good products are absent (developing-world, exotic pairs) — a B+ product beats no product.
- **Discipline over ambition.** Stay inside your circle of competence; a chain shouldn't cosplay as a VC or hedge fund (Arbitrum critique). DAOs fail when they become unhedged hedge funds; they fit immutable, open-source protocols (Uniswap, Morpho).
- **Speed unlocks net-new products.** Real-time blocks enable things impossible elsewhere (Hit One at ~800x leverage; fully on-chain World Markets) — but not everything needs that throughput.
- **AI is capable but green.** Treat it as a limitless supply of smart interns with no life experience; delegate accordingly and beware unqualified self-service in legal/financial/technical domains.

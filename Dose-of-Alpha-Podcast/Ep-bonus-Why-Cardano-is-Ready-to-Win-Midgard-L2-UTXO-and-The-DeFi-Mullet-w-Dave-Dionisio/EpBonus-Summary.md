# Why Cardano is Ready to Win: Midgard L2, UTXO, & The DeFi Mullet — Dose of Alpha Bonus Episode

**Guest:** **Dave Dionisio** — **Midgard Labs**; formerly **Input Output (IOG)**; before that a **bank regulator (CFPB)** and **FS Vector**
**Hosts:** **Brian** and **Eric** (Alpha Growth)
**Context:** Recorded the day Alpha Growth's **Cardano treasury proposal went on-chain**, at the start of "treasury budget season."

## TL;DR

- **Thesis: "We're ready to win."** Cardano now has all the primitives — Dave calls this the most exciting moment in years, with "no excuse" left.
- **Midgard = a Cardano L2 built for UTXO** that **inherits the full Cardano base-layer security model** — no new/separate security assumptions. **Testnet imminent**, currently "quasi-stealth."
- **Vindication narrative:** Ethereum's move toward being "lean" and **exploring UTXO** is treated as validation of what **Charles Hoskinson** mapped out **8–9 years ago**. L2s are described as having been **"parasitic" on Ethereum**, failing to route revenue back to the L1.
- **Cardano governance is a tricameral mini-government**: **SPOs** (validators) + **DReps** (House of Representatives analogue) + a **Constitutional Committee** (Supreme Court analogue), operating under a **Constitution ratified in Buenos Aires** after ~40 global workshops. **Founding entities burned their keys.**
- **The treasury is a sovereign wealth fund** — ~**1.5B ADA**, a **surplus, not $40T of debt** — so spending for growth is defensible.
- **The governance bottleneck is expertise, not democracy:** you cannot ask one delegate to be expert in UI, tokenomics, security, product, GTM, and BD simultaneously. Dave's proposed fix: **peer-delegation and caucusing of experts** rather than abstention.
- **Why Alpha Growth won the RFP:** they offered a **coherent full-ecosystem strategy** (curation + marketing + BD + DeFi), not a single point integration, and were **not competitors** to the teams they'd advise.
- **"If you build it, they will come" is a fallacy** — illustrated by the **Tether Gold** anecdote (great tech, zero marketing/BD/incentives, poor adoption).
- **Bull/bear ranking (inverse of the RedStone episode): DeFi Mullet #1 → Agentic/AI #2 → RWAs #3 (kill).**

### The Overton Window Problem
- Brian/Eric's recurring issue: being **too far ahead of the window** — correct but "not politically correct," because capital and builders need time to catch up.
- Charles Hoskinson framed as suffering the same fate: **brilliant, early, and dismissed**.
- **Now Vitalik is mapping out what Charles described 8–9 years ago** — including **bringing UTXO into Ethereum in modified form** — and "lean Ethereum."
- Dave's reaction: **flattered, applauds it**, doesn't expect the Ethereum community to hand competitors flowers; "more power to him."
- **Stated ambition: join forces with "crypto countrymen"** so the outcome isn't merely a **"centralized bankers' chain"** — while still allowing regulated financial products a place.
- **L2 revenue critique:** Ethereum L2s are **"in some ways parasitic"** on the network. Anecdote — at **Miami**, a large dApp issuer asked *"why isn't the Ethereum price going up?"* The lesson Midgard takes: **if you scale, you must drive revenue back to the L1**, because that is what feeds participants.

### UTXO vs. Account Model (Cardano's Technical Differentiation)
- **No global state to manage** — purely transaction/UTXO-based.
- **Smaller attack surface:** an attacker "would have to crack all of those individuals" instead of one global state that is just a list of accounts.
- **Determinism:** you know exactly what a contract will do, and **you know the exact fee before you submit**.
- **MEV is "not as much of a thing"** as a consequence.
- **Multi-asset transactions:** hundreds of different tokens can move to different places **in one transaction**.
  - Side effect: Cardano feels **"cheated on transaction volume"** metrics — but Dave concedes that **even adjusting for the nuance, the activity isn't there yet.**
- **Native assets = first-class citizens.** A token issued on Cardano **inherits the same properties as ADA itself**, unlike **ERC-20s, which are smart contracts**. This **reduces security risk** and is "nuance we've yet to tap into."
- **State bloat critique of the account model:** everything ever done must be **indexed in perpetuity** — "it does get ugly, it does get clunky." Brian concedes the account model is **easier for people to understand** ("I've got a wallet that I spend") but **transaction-based may be right for long-term scalability**.
- **UTXO is having a moment:** cited adopters/explorers include **Canton** and **Vitalik/Ethereum**.

### Formal Methods & Plutus
- **Plutus** = a **domain-specific language derived from Haskell** — described as "more like math, and very expressive; not a foreign language."
- **Formal verification tooling** compiles functional programs into **another language of math**, letting you **prove with absolute certainty that the thing will not break**. Useful beyond Cardano.
- **Trade-off explicitly acknowledged:** math-based languages make it **very hard for newcomers** to enter; these are "some of the smartest coders on earth in a language most developers couldn't even read."
- **Consequence for DeFi:** that skill set is **not a native financial-services product persona** — hence the stated need for **more capital-markets expertise** in the ecosystem.

### Decentralization — Three Levels (Dave's Framing)
1. **Geographic dispersion of nodes/validators** — the only level most chains (e.g. Solana) consider.
2. **Voting rights that no one controls** — **every ADA carries voting power**; ADA comes with "inalienable rights," you own a piece of the network. Contrast: **owning Bitcoin gives you no say** — miners hold that power.
3. **Who the core creators are** — the **founding entities burned their keys**, and IOG never built it alone (many partners).

### The Bitcoin Coordination Failure (Eric's Argument)
- **2017 upgrade proposal** on Bitcoin driven by **Mike Belshe (BitGo)**, **Tony (BitPay)**, and other large ecosystem players; miners were asked to perform an awkward signaling upgrade.
- **They could not communicate, hold discourse, or coordinate** → **"that to me was the day Bitcoin died."** Eric explicitly still respects Bitcoin: "it got us here."
- **Dave's counter-nuance:** slow upgrades are **"not a bug, it's a feature"** for Bitcoin — but worries about **quantum** and general world-change; "if you can't update to the world quick enough…"
- **Shared conclusion:** the **speed of competition** demands iteration — not 180° turns, but **5% here, 2% there** — to stay competitive. Bitcoin maximalists "assume the world would never innovate, while every other part of society innovates."

### Cardano Governance Architecture
- **Framing:** once you **burn the keys and push the chain into the world**, you cannot have "Lord of the Flies and expect people to coalesce into the ether." There is **~1.5B ADA in a treasury**, accumulated from transactions — **money inside what is inherently a DAO on the ledger**.
- **The Constitution:** built through roughly **40 workshops worldwide**, ratified in a **ceremony in Buenos Aires, Argentina**; Dave **led a satellite event in Nairobi**. It defines **what rights ADA holders have** — "unlike anything that exists anywhere else."
- **Tricameral structure (explicitly analogized to government):**
  - **SPOs — Stake Pool Operators** (validators/miners equivalent): power the network, coordinate on version upgrades. Not dissimilar to other ecosystems.
  - **DReps — Decentralized Representatives** ("Congress / House of Representatives"): ADA holders **delegate voting power without ever losing custody**; the delegate becomes "a politician on this, which comes with the good and the bad."
  - **Constitutional Committee** ("Supreme Court"): arbiter of the Constitution — "if we let Congress just go without the Supreme Court, things go haywire."
- **Missing branch: no executive function.** Dave: *"I don't think any of us want a president of Cardano or a king on top of it"* — **but organization is needed**, and it's already beginning.
  - Expected maturation: appointed **"secretary of the treasury"-type persons or groups**, plus marketing, and functional equivalents of other departments.
  - **Dave's aspiration, said directly to the hosts:** *"Alright, Alpha Growth, please go deliver on this DeFi strategy — that's your executive mandate,"* with DReps nominating such executives.

### The Core Governance Problem: Expertise vs. Breadth
- **Brian's articulation of the challenge:** running a chain or protocol requires **security, UI, product, economic systems, revenue generation, GTM, marketing, BD** — each of which can consume **an entire career and lifetime**.
- Asking a **single delegate to be expert in all of them** — and then to have it all **"ELI5'd"** to them — is an enormous task. Otherwise **"you're talking over everybody's heads every single time."** You cannot hit the right **scale and information density** for clean discourse.
- **Analogy:** saying "I work in finance" is meaningless — **bonds? equities? derivatives?** These are drastically different expertises. Dave applies the same to DeFi: *"I'm not the right guy to decide."*
- **The current reality:** *"It's not one person, one vote — it's truly how much ADA is aligned to you."* Roughly **1,000 DReps**, but **only the top ~100 can move things**; expect further consolidation.
- **Failure mode today: abstention.** Experts in cryptography who know nothing about marketing simply abstain.
- **Dave's proposed fix: peer delegation and caucusing.** Instead of abstaining, a DRep should say *"I trust my peer"* and align their vote with a recognized expert. Not caucusing to **force voting blocks and go to war**, but to **gather experts and rely on each other** — the equivalent of the **House Armed Services Committee** or **Senate Banking**. (Caveat offered: "I also think American politics is a dumpster fire.")

- **Historical aside:** pre-America England had a **three-party check** — **king, lords, church**. You need a king to make **big decisions quickly in war**, delegates to keep the king in check — **but what is the moral authority?** Eric's tentative answer: perhaps **economics and price** are the ultimate moral authority in crypto.
- **Factions Dave already observes bubbling up in Cardano:**
  - **Growth-and-spend party**
  - **Fiscal hawks** — "don't spend one more dollar out of this treasury"
  - **Quasi-socialist** — limit power, push it out to the masses
  - **Nihilists** — "burn the entire Constitution down"

- **Democratic-republic argument (Eric):** you elect people who can **handle sensitive information the general public isn't privy to**; armchair quarterbacking without full information is the failure mode.
- **Encouraging counter-observation (Eric):** as Alpha Growth met Cardano teams, brilliant niche experts proved **readily willing to adapt to new information** and align to a bigger picture — *"I would love to see that happen for more of America in general."*

### Why Alpha Growth Won the Proposal / RFP

- **What they were explicitly avoiding:** a **disjointed strategy** of "plug this one thing in." Inbound integration calls were "off the hook" because **there's money there**.
- **Why not use in-ecosystem teams:** if the advisors are **your competitors**, you don't want to show your cards.
- **The self-aware admission:** *"No one here has driven hundreds of millions of TVL at scale… I don't know what I don't know."*
- **What Alpha Growth offered:** not just **vault curation** or one primitive, but a **coherent, full-ecosystem strategy** spanning marketing and DeFi — able to **plug in anywhere** and give **active help for free** to the DeFi teams. Deciding **who builds which piece and how the pieces fit** is *de facto* the strategy.
- **Alpha Growth's credentials (Brian):** **six years**, helped many chains, **deployed capital into 200+ DeFi protocols** — *"if you're in DeFi, somebody's met with or done a deal with the Alpha Growth team."*
- **The incentives note:** DReps are **volunteering and not getting paid** — Dave flags that **attracting and retaining people** will require rethinking incentives ("look at Congress — I think they make more money with insider trading").
- **The fiscal case to DReps:** *"Cardano has a surplus. It's effectively a sovereign wealth fund. This is not the US and $40 trillion in debt."* Spend for growth if the outcome justifies it.

### The "If You Build It, They Will Come" Fallacy (Tether Gold)
- **~3–4 years ago**, Brian met a very senior person at **Tether**, who said: *"We have a problem — we launched Tether Gold and we're not getting the adoption we want."*
- Asked about **incentive campaign, marketing, BD** — answer: **"We're not doing any of that."** Why? **"We didn't have to do that for Tether."**
- **The lesson:** Tether had **inherent, pent-up product-market-fit demand** — one of the **first CDPs**, letting Bitcoin holders print dollars to hedge exposure. **That is very, very rare.**
- **Everyone else needs marketing, BD, and a points program.** *"The people who engineer and make the F1 cars are not the same people who drive it"* — specialization matters.
- **Counter-example cited: Ethena** ran a "crazy points program"; token launched at roughly a **$700M market cap**.
- **The bad reasoning pattern:** pointing at Bitcoin or Ethereum and asking "why do we need to do X, Y, Z? They didn't." **All new chains do.**

### Culture, Ethos, and Philosophy
- **Dave on why Cardano is fun:** a **grand experiment** where "the wheels are off" — if a government wants to shut Cardano down, **good luck reaching ~2,500–3,000 nodes in different countries** ("there's probably some in Antarctica"). **Libertarian and cypherpunk** by nature; attracts people motivated by **distrust of institutions** and **distaste for political polarization**. *"It's not an actual nation, but it feels like that, where everyone can play their part."*
- **Nostalgia for 2017–2018:** many companies, personalities, and focuses **all contributing to a shared ecosystem** without needing to agree; **no negative defecting** — anything built is a new opportunity.
- **Eric's civilization thesis:** it takes **brilliance, trust, and delayed gratification**. The **DRep system builds trust** because **votes are public and on-chain**; Alpha Growth has "been doxxed forever."
  - People willing to accept **delayed gratification** are usually **driven by being right and pursuing truth rather than pursuing money** — and that builds **wealth rather than just revenue**.
  - Observation: **many Cardano founders had commercial success outside crypto** and put it aside to build on ideology — "a lot of philosophers, a lot of ethos-driven people."
- **Eric's role framing:** *"I would prefer to be the philosopher, but I get paid to be the pragmatist"* — happy to take the steps toward a vision set by people planning 10, 15, 20 years out, versus those who **extract and leave in two years**.

### Bull/Bear Ranking (F/Marry/Kill on AI, RWAs, DeFi Mullet)
- **#1 — DeFi Mullet.** "Business in front, party in the back": **centralized-exchange custodians up front, DeFi in the back.**
  - Likes that trustworthy custodians exist — **so long as the "party in the back" is not fully abandoned.**
  - The core desire: *"We just want people to be able to interact with their own money without permission control."* KYC can exist, but **not a world of surveillance and CBDCs**.

  - **Key blocker: identity.** *"We're going to need some level of identity that controls all my agents; otherwise they're just kind of up"* — a **security concern**.
  - Notes **Alpaca** and **Robinhood** already enabling **agentic trading from an LLM harness**.
- **#3 — RWAs (the "kill").** Expects RWAs to attract **the most capital**, and **applauds real assets coming on-chain**, but personally ranks them last: *"I just don't know how necessary it is, and if people will want to go use those"* if they're already holding the asset in an app. Concedes **"the Ondo people would tell me how wrong I am."**
- **Note:** this is the **exact inverse** of the RedStone guest's ranking (RWA #1, DeFi Mullet #2, AI killed).

### Privacy & Surveillance
- **The binary endgame (Eric):** either **your personal agent manages your finances non-custodially** while your curated AIs take the risk and positioning — **or** you delegate to a **centralized exchange / RIA / money manager**. Dave: *"Maybe it's not either/or — it's both, dependent upon your own value system."*
- **Dave's personal stance:** *"I want to trade every stock myself. I want full privacy."* Self-described as unusual in this.
- **Rebuttal to "I have nothing to hide" (Eric):** *"I ask them for their mother's maiden name, their social security number, and all their credit card information. Not a single one has given it to me — which makes me just not trust them."*
- **Insider observation:** at the bank regulator, **"we were collecting more data second only to the NSA"** — every credit card transaction, freely handed over by banks.
- **The DOJ case study:** a payment processor was going to be acquired by **Tencent and Alibaba**; the **DOJ blocked it** because IP-address and geolocation data on all card transactions reveals, over time, **exactly when a senator's child goes to the grocery store**. "Seems benign. But yeah — it's creepy."
- **The acknowledged tension:** *"I need these AI tools to be as powerful as they can be for me,"* while knowing Google, Facebook, or the government **"know me better than me."* Dave asked an AI to summarize how the workday is actually spent and found the result **"freaky."**
- **Money as the last bastion:** *"Am I allowed to hand someone a $20 bill in a field where no one's watching, or should every transaction be drawn in that field?"*
- **Cardano's answer:** you can build **fully permissioned side chains** for a nation state or business — *"I want everyone to do everything they want to do."*

### What Success Looks Like for Cardano
- **Near-term: an absolute killer dApp — plural — that people can use with confidence.**
- **Security is the gate:** *"We can't have these hacks that are rampant on EVM and other chains."* Money put in should make more money **provided the thing doesn't get hacked**; the **amount of capital leaving DeFi because of hacks is concerning**.
- **Every participating protocol should leave with a roadmap or guidance** on how to improve and compete, and on **finding differentiation in Cardano's model — not just UTXO**.
- **Rebutting the "ghost chain" / "cult" critique:** people **love participating in this ecosystem** — the missing piece is to **"crank the ratchet of the economic engine"** toward the TVL and transaction goals so **more life flows through the system that feeds the builders**.
- **Cardano need not be the high-frequency trading chain** — *"it is open and pure, and that's what makes it beautiful."*

### The AI Segment (post-guest riff, with Dave staying on)
- Framed around the **last day of Claude "Fable"** availability.
- **Brian's use case:** stayed up until **3 a.m.** asking what the **event space looks like in 2030 and 2040** for a friend running an event app — "one of the most mind-blowing conversations." Recommends: pick something you're nerdy about, chat **30 minutes**, then **challenge it and go back and forth**. Ran out of credits mid-session.
- **Why it's compelling:** it **aggregates data across disparate viewpoints** you never considered, enabling discourse a single human couldn't produce.
- **Dave's initial skepticism:** wondered if Fable was **hyped / a marketing move from Anthropic**, and drew a parallel to **incumbent regulatory capture** (funding anti-AI groups). Verdict: **"truly phenomenal"** for those couple of days, then taken away; feels "a little bit softer now."
- **The trajectory argument:** current models are, per **Sam Altman**, *"the dumbest model you'll ever use today"* — "which is terrifying." Dave sees today's systems as **capturing and compressing the world's data (as Google did)**, but expects **parabolic gains once systems learn and generate synthetic data**, citing AI **cracking unsolved math problems**.
- **The prompting shift:** *"You don't have to prompt it as crisp anymore… if you give it a broader task, it's not a human brain, so it can find connectivity we would natively not have tinkered with forever."*
- **AI as governance arbiter:** Dave sees potential for AI to **arbitrate disputes** ("that's your opinion") — while flagging **training-data bias** ("ask 100 lawyers a question, get 100 answers"). Cites **Palantir**-style approaches: **run every model 10 times on one prompt and smash the outputs together to find the truth.**
  - **Applied concretely:** Dave and Phil DeSarro **published what they stand for** as policy principles, so any proposal can be **gap-analyzed against them** — "it at least does the legwork for us."
- **The alignment thesis:** deep delegation and governance ultimately hinge on **what vision you can get enough people around**. *"Human ingenuity combined with AI can get anything done, but we have to align on that goal."* What is the **North Star metric / KPI**? Agree on that, and you can iterate to the right conclusion again and again.
- **Moon-landing analogy (Eric):** the **Navy and Air Force coordinated without orders** to get a satellite atop a Hawaiian mountain because it was "for the moon landing" — cutting through political nonsense via a shared mission.

### The Ceramics Parable (closing argument)
- Told to Eric by a **21-year-old** at a startup in **2013–2014**.
- A **ceramics guru** split a class in two: one half graded on their **3 best pots** at year's end; the other on their **top 300 pots**.
- The "3 pots" group was **meticulous** — perfecting every line and stroke. The "300 pots" group **just kept spinning pots**.
- **Result: the 300-pot group produced far superior work in every capacity.**
- **Moral: "Shots on goal — you become what you practice."** Making perfect the enemy of the good means tinkering forever; **Brian Armstrong succeeded on his ~10th venture**.
- **Closing call to action:** the proposal is live on Cardano for a few weeks to a month — go vote, *"because we're hoping to come in and take more shots on goal. We've built a lot of pots."*

## Key Takeaways / Conclusions

- **Midgard's core claim is a security claim, not a throughput claim.** Most L2s ask users to accept a new trust model; Midgard's pitch is that it **inherits Cardano's base-layer security wholesale** — leaning on a chain that has **never gone down in ten years** as the differentiator.
- **The UTXO argument is about failure modes, not features.** No global state means a **smaller attack surface**, **determinism**, **known fees pre-submission**, and **structurally less MEV** — properties that matter more as institutional and regulated capital arrives.
- **Cardano's real bottleneck is commercial, not technical.** The chain has world-class formal-methods engineers writing in a language most developers can't read — which is **not a financial-services product persona**. The gap is **capital-markets and go-to-market expertise**, which is precisely what the Alpha Growth proposal is buying.
- **Governance is failing on expertise, not on decentralization.** With **~1,000 DReps but only ~100 that matter**, and experts abstaining outside their domain, the system needs **specialized committees and peer delegation** — the crypto equivalent of Senate Banking — plus an **executive function nobody wants to call a king**.
- **"If you build it, they will come" is the most expensive assumption in crypto.** Tether Gold had the technology and the brand and still underperformed without marketing, BD, or incentives — because **Tether's original PMF was an anomaly, not a template**.
- **The treasury argument reframes spending as investment.** Cardano runs a **surplus sovereign wealth fund**, not a deficit — so the DRep question shifts from "can we afford this?" to "does the outcome justify it?"
- **Ethereum's convergence on UTXO is the strategic tailwind.** If the largest smart-contract ecosystem is now borrowing Cardano's architectural premises, the window for Cardano to claim the **non-permissioned UTXO settlement layer** — and bridge to permissioned ones like Canton — is open now.
- **The panel's narrative rankings directly conflict across episodes.** This guest ranks **DeFi Mullet first and RWAs last**; the RedStone guest ranked **RWAs first and AI last** — a useful signal that these narratives are being priced very differently depending on where a builder sits in the stack.
- **Privacy is treated as the load-bearing value, not a feature.** The through-line from bank regulation ("second only to the NSA") to the DOJ/Tencent case to CBDC fears is that **money is the last surveillance-free bastion** — and permissionless self-custody is what the "party in the back" of the DeFi Mullet must preserve.
- **Execution philosophy: volume beats perfection.** The ceramics parable, "shots on goal," and Brian Armstrong's tenth venture all argue the same thing — **align on a single North Star KPI and iterate relentlessly**, rather than waiting for a complete strategy that thousands of stakeholders will never agree on.

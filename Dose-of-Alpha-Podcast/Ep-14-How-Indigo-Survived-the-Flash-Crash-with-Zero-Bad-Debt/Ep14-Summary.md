# How Indigo Survived the Flash Crash with Zero Bad Debt — Dose of Alpha Ep. 14

**Guest:** **Eric Coley** — Foundation Director and core contributor, **Indigo Protocol** (CDP / synthetic assets on Cardano)
**Hosts:** **Brian Coligan** and **Eric** (Alpha Growth)
**Context:** Recorded while the **Alpha Growth "Prime" proposal** was live for Cardano DRep voting, alongside a pending **Net Change Limit (NCL)** budget vote.

## TL;DR

- **Indigo took zero bad debt through the October 10 flash crash — and has taken none to this day.** No solvency issues, peg maintained, and **stability pool depositors "did really, really well."**
- **10/10 was not novel to Indigo** — it had already survived prior ADA-specific and market-wide flash crashes, and **endured each event better than the one before** as parameters were added across V1 → V2.
- **Indigo V3 shipped a multicollateral suite:** any non-ADA collateral can be **governance-approved and whitelisted with its own unique parameter set per asset market** — explicitly **setting the stage for RWAs as collateral**.
- **"Tokenize everything" has been Indigo's mantra since day one — before Larry Fink said it.** Synthetic assets are natively synergistic with RWAs since they already mirror real-world assets.
- **The Indigo Innovation Proposal (a tokenized T-bill solution for Cardano) failed** in Cardano governance, along with most other commercial proposals. Eric intends to keep proposing it — **tokenized T-bills are the fastest-growing segment within RWAs**.
- **Cardano's structural weakness is inbound user acquisition, not culture.** *"A lot of the Cardano enthusiasts are probably not going to leave the Cardano walls. But where we've been lacking is bringing users here from other places."*
- **Two founder fallacies named:** (1) *"if you build it, they will come"* / *"if the app's good enough it'll automatically have $100M TVL"*; (2) **the people who build it are the people who can drive it** — there are roughly **27 disciplines** you'd need to be world-class at.
- **F/Marry/Kill verdict: kill the DeFi Mullet; AI/agentic finance is here to stay; RWAs will pump then normalize fast.**
- **Post-interview thesis: the CDP is the core primitive of all of DeFi** — Tether was effectively a proto-CDP, MakerDAO/Sky is ~$7–8B — and **CDPs, not increasingly isolated money markets, have the room to become the next Aave-scale protocol.**

### Black Swans and Antifragility (Nassim Taleb)
- **Brian's definition of a Black Swan event:** **leverage compounds** and keeps getting **incrementally more efficient over time until something breaks** — and when it breaks, it **breaks the entire stack of leverage and completely rebases and resets the economy**.
- **2008 is the reference case** — and many argue it **never actually finished** because of **the bailouts and hyperinflation**; had it played all the way out it **would have gone significantly further**.
- **The DeFi implication:** in these events, having **permissionless decentralized protocols that don't accumulate bad debt and remain solvent** is remarkable.
- **Alpha Growth's own record:** as market curators, **none of their money markets collected bad debt** — *"not a given, because we try to be decently risk-on."*
- **The wider industry did not fare as well:** *"A lot of curators weren't safe,"* and **many market makers still have holes on their balance sheets that they won't permissionlessly make available.**
- **Practical takeaway:** if you expect a Black Swan, the **stability pool is a good place to park capital** — you earn from it while helping protect the system.
- **The antifragility bridge (Brian):** Taleb followed *The Black Swan* with ***Antifragile*** — **the best investments get stronger the more they are tested**, like **lifting weights** on the human body. **Indigo is described as the antithesis of the Black Swan: more antifragile every cycle.**

### Is the Framework Becoming Obsolete? (Volatility as the New Norm)
- **Eric Coley's provocation:** he wonders whether Taleb's theory is being **"somewhat rendered obsolete"** by how global markets are behaving.
- **The evidence:** *"I never thought I would see precious metals markets or crude oil start to look like a crypto chart."* Brian: *"They pumped and dumped the periodic table on us."*
- **A reversal of his own long-held view:** he **used to believe crypto would mature into looking like traditional global markets**. Now he wonders if the **opposite** is happening — **volatility is the new norm**, **big money can capitalize on it**, and **global markets may start to look more like crypto markets**.
- **Related structural driver (Brian):** **liquidation hunting** in permissionless, open-source DEXs and perp markets showed people that **they could do this to brokers and giant market makers** — the same dynamic as **GameStop and AMC**. *"A big tell for a lot of people as far as what was possible by the little man squeezing the big man."*

### Information Cascades (Brian's Framework)
- The reason commodities now trade like crypto charts is **the attention economy compounding with an information cascade**.
- **The reflexive core:** *"If everybody believes we're going to come out of the four-year cycle in October, then we're coming out of the four-year cycle in October."*
- **The aggregation of information creates a cascade on itself.**
- **The polling analogy:** *"Who would you want to have a beer and a burger with at a bar"* predicts elections better than *"who do you think will win"* — and the **most accurate question is the derivative: "who do you think other people will vote for?"**
- **The insight: what you believe matters less than what you believe others believe.**
- **Eric Coley's counter:** *"How many times are we seeing it play out where what everyone thinks is going to happen is the exact opposite?"* He is **not treating October as a definitive four-year-cycle event**.

### RWAs, Multicollateral, and Indigo's Roadmap
- **"Tokenize everything" has been Indigo's slogan since day one** — **before Larry Fink said it**. *"I should have probably trademarked that."*
- **Why RWAs are natively synergistic with Indigo:** its **synthetic assets already mirror real-world assets**, so **moving upstream to capture the RWA target market makes structural sense**.
- **The Indigo Innovation Proposal** — submitted in the Cardano governance phase to bring a **tokenized T-bill solution to Cardano** — **failed**, as did **a lot of other commercial proposals**. Eric intends to **keep proposing it**, arguing **every serious ecosystem needs to consider it** and that **tokenized T-bills are the number one growing RWA within the RWA sector**.
- **Indigo V3's multicollateral suite is the enabling technology:**
  - **Any new or alternative form of collateral beyond ADA** can go through **governance**, be **approved and whitelisted**, and carry **its own unique parameter set into every individual asset market**.
  - *"Indigo V3 really sets the stage for a future and world where an RWA could be used as collateral."*
- **Brian's structured-product vision — the Strike partnership:**
  - **The basis trade:** take out **SpaceX** exposure, **borrow iUSD** against it, and run **SpaceX perps on Strike**, managing the funding rate up and down.
  - *"That leveraged loop and basis funding rate is, to me, the future of CDPs and the RWA markets."*
  - **Extended recipe: iUSD + Strike + DEX pool liquidity + an isolated lending market** → *"we have the recipe for a very nice structured product… whether it goes up or down in value, the thing will just continuously print on the volatility."*
- **The cost-of-dollars problem that motivates all this:** two days prior, an **LP quoted a 16% hurdle rate** for stables deployed into DEX liquidity. *"That's crazy high. Even in this market, that's insane"* — **even with RWA backing**, not meme-coin backing.
- **Why more collateral types is strictly better (Brian):**
  1. **Print dollars out of nothing** — cheaper than finding a counterparty; enables **carry trades** where holding longer gives a **lower borrow rate than a money market**.
  2. **Service clients better.**
  3. **Lower macroeconomic depeg risk** — you are **no longer dependent on a single collateral type or a single bubble**; risk is diversified and spread, which makes **stability pools easier to leverage**.
  - **On the "you're expanding the attack surface" objection: that's actually a good thing**, provided you have **stability pools providing peg maintenance and absorbing volatility**.

### The Case for the Prime Proposal
- **The core need: an acceleration program.** *"The DeFi ecosystem has been mainnet now for four years and some change. We've seen a lot of great developers come and go, a lot of great ideas. But we're still hamstrung by mission-critical DeFi infrastructure."*
- **What's required:** someone to come in, **identify the gaps developers already know exist**, and run **a robust program to fill them**.
- **The specific blocker is composability.** The infrastructure gaps **prevent composability plays that other LPs and DeFi desks treat as a baseline** before they'll park money in an ecosystem. *"We don't have it, or we have MVPs of it."*
- **The counterfactual:** without it, *"we continue to do what hasn't worked over the course of the next couple of years — and I'm afraid that just won't cut it."*
- **Credit given: a shout-out to "the Pentad"** and others for delivering things the ecosystem had needed for a long time.
- **The verdict:** they can remain competitive, **but to really level up they need to be bold and take the next step.**

### The Two Fallacies (Brian)
- **Fallacy #1 — "We have the best tech, so if you build it, they will come."** This is **only true without competition**: if you're the **sole place to lend and borrow against tokens**, or hold **the only on/off-ramps and payment rails**, or have a **genuine first-mover unlock**. *"But there are a lot of L1s, a lot of L2s out there. They become competitive."*
- **Fallacy #2 — "The same people who build it are the ones who can drive it."** The **race car analogy**: there are **drivers, pit crew, advisors, tire mechanics**. Brian once wrote out the list — roughly **27 different things** you must be expert at, nearly best in the world, to make a protocol **independently successful without help**: UI, security, website, DAOs, tokenomics and utility, and more.
- **The founder blind spot:** community members and especially **naive first-time founders** think a good enough app **automatically gets $100M in TVL**. They **don't recognize the disconnect between building the app and what family offices, liquid funds, and institutional deployment actually require** — and **how it must be packaged** for them.
- **The reframe:** you may already have enough infrastructure to be a core piece of that package — *"but what they're looking for requires more pieces than you even have within your power to control. And that shouldn't negatively impact you."* What's needed is **a bird's-eye view and more interoperability**, using **the great pieces that already exist in Cardano** and adding **a couple of tweaks and pieces where needed**.

### Cardano's Insularity Problem
- **The most compelling reason for an initiative like Prime (Eric Coley):** Cardano has been **"ultra-siloed"** with a fabulous community and culture, but **"a lot of the Cardano enthusiasts are probably not going to leave the Cardano walls,"** whereas other chains see **more cross-chain movement in their user bases**.
- **The persistent gap: bringing users in from other places.**
- **How an acceleration program fixes it — "monkey see, monkey do":** once people see, **post-proposal**, that **there is more to do here**, and as the **numbers ratchet up**, outsiders start to look over. **Organic growth follows the initiative.**

### Post-Interview Riff: Why the CDP Is DeFi's Core Primitive
- **CDPs were the first DeFi primitive that truly took off.** People forget there was **EtherDelta** ("eth delta") as an early DEX — *"but the first thing people really wanted to do was hedge Ethereum or go long Ethereum."*
- **The second thing people wanted was to trade in dollars** — which is **how Tether came to be** as centralized exchanges struggled to get dollars on-chain.
- **Tether as a proto-CDP:** originally you **put in Bitcoin, hedged, and got out Tether**, backed by **Bitfinex** — then one of the largest exchanges — giving Tether **embedded utility on that venue**. *"That's kind of like a CDP, but that wasn't DeFi."*
- **Tether redemption critique (stated with care):** *"I'm not a Tether truther, but there's no way to redeem Tether… the only way is by going through an exchange and switching to another asset."* Allegedly **some market makers can** — the hosts **do not hold those permissions** and can only speculate.
- **MakerDAO coined the term** collateralized debt position on the DeFi side and made minting against collateral real. **MakerDAO / Sky is now roughly $7–8 billion**, with its own money markets (**Spark**).
- **The recurring pushback they hear from ecosystem leads:** *"Why are you guys so hyped about a CDP? It's not that interesting."* The rebuttal: *"It's the only way you can print your own currency, have it decentralized, and completely back it with your own token throughout the entire ecosystem — and whether it goes up or down, Indigo remains solvent."*
- **Why Alpha Growth is more bullish on CDPs than most of DeFi:** they've **run large-scale strategies** and **proven CDPs are more cost-effective when held for long periods**.
- **Why others prefer money markets:** they're **faster**, and it's **easier for curators to interject themselves**.
- **The structural argument for CDPs:** as **money markets get more and more isolated**, you become **increasingly dependent on unutilized margin**, which **becomes a limiting agent**. *"It's hard to re-hypothecate if you can't have big enough trades go through."* **Isolation decouples risk, but you need big flows to be taken seriously.**
- **The Aave/Twitter analogy:** *"Aave, if it were built today, would not get a ton of adoption — kind of like Twitter."* When Twitter open-sourced its algorithm everyone assumed it was over, *"but the value of Twitter was never its algorithm; it was the other users. The value of Aave isn't their tech — it's the TVL they already have."* People keep using it **because they know they can withdraw** — at high utilization *"you can still withdraw $25 million."*
- **The conclusion: CDPs still have room to become the next Aave-scale protocol in a way money markets don't**, because money markets are **getting smaller and more isolated**.

## Key Takeaways / Conclusions

- **The headline claim is falsifiable and specific: zero bad debt, ever — including through 10/10.** Indigo's argument is not that it avoided stress but that it **has been stressed repeatedly** (ADA-specific and market-wide flash crashes) and **performed better each time** as parameters accumulated across V1 → V2.
- **Stability pools are the mechanism, not a marketing feature.** Depositors supply the liquidity that lets liquidations clear without generating bad debt, and **they are compensated for it** — which is why "park capital in the stability pool ahead of a Black Swan" is a coherent strategy rather than a pitch.
- **Antifragility is the organizing metaphor of the episode, and the guest immediately complicates it.** Eric Coley's willingness to ask whether **Taleb's framework is being obsoleted by permanent volatility** — with gold and crude now charting like crypto — is the most intellectually honest moment in the conversation.
- **Indigo V3's multicollateral suite is the strategically load-bearing release.** Everything about the RWA ambition — tokenized T-bills, SpaceX basis trades, structured products on Strike — depends on **non-ADA collateral being governance-whitelisted with per-asset parameters**.
- **The 16% hurdle rate for stables is the market's verdict on Cardano DeFi's current risk profile** — and the clearest quantitative argument for why **collateral diversification and composability infrastructure are urgent**, not aspirational.
- **Cardano's growth problem is asymmetric: strong retention, weak acquisition.** A culture that keeps its enthusiasts inside the walls is also a culture that doesn't pull users in from other chains — which is precisely the gap an external, "unburdened" operator is being hired to close.
- **The two fallacies generalize well beyond Cardano.** "Best tech wins" only holds without competition, and the **~27 disciplines** required for independent protocol success mean **most teams structurally cannot self-drive** — the builder/driver split is a real division of labor, not a slight.
- **The guest's endorsement is credible precisely because he disclaimed self-interest.** Answering a DRep's "how do you know this benefits Indigo?" with **"I don't"** — and grounding support in ecosystem-level rather than protocol-level upside — is the strongest form the argument could take.
- **The CDP thesis is the episode's most contrarian analytical position.** While most of DeFi has moved toward isolated money markets, the argument here is that **isolation caps flow size and re-hypothecation**, and that **CDPs — the primitive behind Tether's origin and MakerDAO/Sky's $7–8B — retain the headroom to reach Aave scale.**
- **The Aave/Twitter analogy is the sharpest reusable insight:** incumbent moats in DeFi are **TVL and users, not technology**. A protocol built today with better tech loses to one with worse tech and $25M of withdrawable depth.
- **Narrative ranking inverts the two prior episodes:** this guest **kills the DeFi Mullet** and **backs agentic AI**, where the RedStone guest killed AI and backed RWAs, and the Midgard guest backed the Mullet and killed RWAs — three builders, three incompatible rankings, each anchored in where they sit in the stack.
